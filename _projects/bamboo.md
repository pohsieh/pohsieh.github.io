## Abstract

Despite the rapid development of building technologies, low-tech construction methods using local materials are still preferred in resource-limited areas. The question addressed in this research is how design computation can be integrated into a localized building process. The research was conducted with an architectural practice on the NYCU campus in Hsinchu, an area rich in bamboo.

Given the contextual challenges of limited time and technical expertise, the research develops a low-tech, tectonic framework for bamboo structures: (1) form-finding iterations combining reciprocal frame and tensegrity structures; (2) a cable-strut connection that uses local resources and the inherent flexibility of bamboo culms; (3) a decoupled assembly process supported by computational tools; and (4) practical application in the final project.
