## Abstract

Shotcrete 3D concrete printing (SC3DCP) offers vast potential for digital fabrication and structural engineering. However, it faces limitations with complex geometries involving non-developable surfaces. To address these challenges from a computational perspective, this research introduces an adjustable formwork system based on a discrete interlocking mechanism.

The proposed model consists of an octagonal, porous unit inspired by chainmail. Using the chainmail pattern and interlocking parts, the formwork (1) offers a wide range of flexibility at each node, so it can be applied to various types of curved surfaces; (2) can be reused over time by adjusting each interlocking part; and (3) can be printed in place without external supports, reducing assembly complexity compared with traditional engineered formwork. Overall, the research aims to reduce geometric constraints in construction through a novel, practical method.
